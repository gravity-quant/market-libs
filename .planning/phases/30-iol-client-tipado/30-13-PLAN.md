<objective>
Close the three bypasses the fifth verification cycle found in the lock that 30-11 widened.

30-11 took `_raw_exception_renders` from 3 flagged shapes to 11 and replaced the renderer name-match
with a shape-based census. A fresh pass found three more shapes the widening did not anticipate:
`getattr(exc, "message"/"args", …)` slips through because the `getattr` exemption is keyed on the
callee name and never looks at the attribute argument; `exc.__dict__` is missing from the leaky set
entirely; and the census's read-predicate lacks both the `%`-formatting rule its sibling detector
already implements and any notion of generic delegation, so a `%`-format or `print(exc)`-shaped
second renderer passes undetected.

Purpose: no live leak exists in `main_iol.py` today — this is durability, not disclosure. But the
lock is the mechanism that keeps the CR-01/CR-02 class closed after this phase ends, and it has now
acquired new blind spots at every widening. `getattr(exc, "message", "")` is a one-token edit away
from any of the 32 sanctioned report sites, and unlike the direct attribute spelling it currently
reads as *deliberately allowed* rather than merely unchecked.

Output: a `getattr`-aware leaky-attribute rule keyed on the attribute name, `__dict__` in the leaky
set, and a census read-predicate that shares the `%`-format rule with its sibling and adds generic
delegation — each with a positive case pinning the newly-closed bypass and a negative case pinning
the shape that must stay allowed. Test surface only: no driver code, no package code.
</objective>
